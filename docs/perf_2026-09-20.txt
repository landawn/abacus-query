  The measurements used the following conditions and interpretation rules:

  - Five fresh JVMs per version per suite, with nine measured rounds per workload. Results use the median of each
    JVM’s rounds, then the median across JVMs.

  - One benchmark JVM ran at a time. Compilation and tests finished before timing. The original harnesses, warmup,
    calibration, and deterministic case shuffling were preserved.

  - ParsedSql and SqlParser used -Xms512m -Xmx1024m -XX:+UseG1GC. Builders used -Xms128m -Xmx256m -XX:+UseG1GC.
  - Current uses abacus-common 8.0.1, previous snapshots 8.0.0, and 965f6afe 7.9.3. These results include dependency
    changes and do not isolate individual fixes.

  - Current and previous snapshots produced matching outputs in every measured case. Nine ParsedSql, two SqlParser,
    and six builder cases differed at 965f6afe; those cases were excluded from equivalent-work baseline aggregates.

  - Small changes and overlapping timing ranges should not be treated as demonstrated regressions or optimizations.
    These are standalone benchmarks on a shared host, not JMH measurements or application-throughput estimates.

  - Cache-hit measurements were uncontended and excluded initial string hashing. Allocation figures exclude
    background-thread allocations and do not measure retained heap.

  - Artifact and dependency fingerprints passed verification. Production and unit-test sources remained unchanged.

  The complete comparison CSV (docs/performance/rerun-2026-09-20/comparison.csv) contains all 321 workloads,
  including improvements, smaller differences, allocation figures, and per-version timing ranges.
